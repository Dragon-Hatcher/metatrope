    How to play

    English is full of compound words whose two halves were borrowed whole from another language. Metatrope takes such a word, splits it, translates each half into a different language, and glues it back together.

    Your job is to name the original. Take the game's own name:

    metatrope

    The languagesLatin → Greek
    The splitmeta · trope
    meta‑Greek for across — as in metaphor
    ‑tropeGreek for turn, change — as in heliotrope
    So the English wordis built from Latin for across + change…

    transmute  (Latin trans + mūtāre)

    Hints

    Four levels, each opened by the one above it, giving away a little more each time. Fewer is better, but there is no penalty beyond your own pride — and the share card.

    • The coinage — the two languages, and the split showing where the seam between the roots falls. Both open from the start.
    • Clue relatives — other English words built on the root as it appears in the clue. charta gives you cartography and charter, which is how you work out that it means paper.
    • Meanings — what the root actually means, said plainly.
    • Answer relatives — other English words built on the root in the original language. This is the last resort: for a clue meaning horse + river, being handed hippodrome and hippocampus leaves very little to guess.

    The two columns are the two roots, so you can dig into whichever half has you stuck without paying for the other. Some roots came into English alone and left no cousins behind — those puzzles simply have fewer hints.

    Guessing

    Guess as often as you like. Wrong guesses cost nothing but the count on your share card.

    New puzzle every day at midnight, your time.
